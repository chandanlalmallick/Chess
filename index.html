How to Play

Objective

Checkmate your opponent's king — trap it so it cannot escape capture.

How Pieces Move

  • Pawn: Moves forward one square (two on its first move), captures diagonally.
  • Knight: Moves in an "L" shape and can jump over pieces.
  • Bishop: Moves any distance diagonally.
  • Rook: Moves any distance horizontally or vertically.
  • Queen: Moves any distance in any direction.
  • King: Moves one square in any direction.

Check & Checkmate

"Check" means the king is under attack. If the king cannot escape check, it's "checkmate" and the game ends.

Castling

A special move where the king moves two squares toward a rook, and the rook moves to the square the king crossed. Allowed only if neither piece has moved, the squares between are empty, and the king is not in, through, or into check.

En Passant

If an enemy pawn moves two squares and lands beside your pawn, you may capture it as if it had moved only one square, but only immediately on the next move.

Promotion

When a pawn reaches the far end of the board, it must be promoted to a queen, rook, bishop, or knight.

Draws

A game is drawn by stalemate (no legal moves, not in check), insufficient material, threefold repetition, the fifty-move rule, or agreement between players.
